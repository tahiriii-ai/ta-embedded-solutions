Parcel-Tracking System: Beacon + Gateway

Full product development · 2 PCBs · Enclosure · KiCad + Fusion 360 · Client project (Fiverr)

Two ESP32 boards and a printable enclosure for a parcel-tracking system, taken from the client's specification to files ready for manufacturing.

01The product

A client needed custom hardware for a delivery-driver system: small battery-powered beacons that clip onto parcels and flash and beep on command, and a gateway the driver carries. The phone app talks to the gateway over Bluetooth LE, and the gateway talks to the beacons over ESP-NOW.

The client already had working prototypes and firmware. The job was to turn both devices into professional, production-oriented custom PCBs, with an enclosure for the beacon.

Mobile appBLE · Gateway (ESP32-S3)ESP-NOW · Beacons (ESP32-C3)

02What I delivered

  • Beacon PCB. 4-layer ESP32-C3 board with a complete manufacturing package.
  • Beacon enclosure. Five 3D-printable parts, with STL, STEP, editable Fusion 360 files, drawings and an assembly animation.
  • Gateway PCB. 4-layer ESP32-S3 board bridging BLE and ESP-NOW, with schematic, layout and production files.
  • Both boards. Editable KiCad projects, BOM, Gerbers, drill files and pick-and-place files.

03How the work ran

Both devices were developed in milestones, and the client reviewed each stage before the next one began.

  1. Spec review. I reviewed both technical specifications and sent written recommendations on the power architecture, the switchable 5 V LED rail, antenna keep-out and the ESP-NOW wake/sleep strategy before any design began.
  2. Milestone plan. The project was split into phases. Each phase was delivered and reviewed before the next began, which is how the client asked to work.
  3. Schematic first. Every schematic went to the client as a PDF and a KiCad file, and was reviewed with them before layout started.
  4. Placement before routing. The client reviewed the Beacon placement and 3D views before I routed a single track.
  5. Verify, then deliver. ERC and DRC were run clean before hand-off, and each package was delivered as one organized folder.
  6. Close every gap. The client audited the Beacon package against a checklist. Every item was closed and the files were reissued as one clean ZIP.

04Beacon PCB

A compact ESP32-C3 board with four addressable RGB LEDs, a passive buzzer, USB-C and a LiPo charger with power-path management, plus two gold contacts on the back for a charging dock. About 48.8 × 33.8 mm on four layers.

07Where client review improved the design

The client reviewed every schematic closely. These are real changes that came out of that review:

  • Smaller buzzer. Swapped a 22 × 22 × 10.5 mm part for a 12 × 12 × 8.5 mm passive transducer to keep the Beacon compact. It stays externally driven, so firmware can play beeps, double beeps and siren patterns.
  • USB data-line protection. Added a dedicated low-capacitance ESD device (USBLC6-2SC6) on the USB-C D+ and D− lines.
  • Specific parts, not placeholders. Replaced a generic diode with a named SS34 Schottky, with footprint and datasheet.
  • Gateway status LED. Replaced the first RGB LED with a WS2812B-2020-V6 after the client questioned its 3.3 V supply range.
  • Gateway controls. Corrected a slide-switch footprint to a tactile button, removed a duplicate battery connector, added separate BOOT and RESET buttons, and re-enabled all ERC checks.
  • Communication standard. When the client fixed ESP-NOW as the link, I checked the finished Beacon against it before continuing. No hardware change was needed and nothing had been ordered.
