01The brief

The client has a small ESP32-C3 beacon that clips onto a parcel and talks to a paired gateway over ESP-NOW. The PCB carries four RGB LEDs, a piezo buzzer, a replaceable Li-Po battery, a USB-C port and two pogo-pin charging pads for a dock.

It needed a 3D-printable housing that shows the LED status clearly, holds a parcel clip, and lets the battery be replaced without soldering. This enclosure is one part of a two-device system; the PCBs are covered in the full product case study.

02My role

  • Enclosure design around the supplied PCB and the chosen Li-Po 503035 battery, using their exact STEP models
  • Five-part print design: lower housing, diffuser lid, clip bracket, clip jaw, battery retainer
  • Interference and clearance checks between every part, the PCB, battery, contacts and clip
  • Print-ready STL and STEP for each part, one full print plate, editable Fusion 360 files
  • Dimensioned drawings, bill of materials, manufacturing notes and an assembly animation

03Design decisions

  • Light through the lid. The lid is a translucent diffuser with a 1.2 mm top plate, so the four RGB LEDs glow across the whole top face. A smoked or mirror-film finish is also described for a dark look when the LEDs are off.
  • Dark lower shell. The black housing holds the PCB on its four existing M2 mounting holes, so the board needs no extra brackets.
  • One screw type. Four identical M2 × 20 screws go in from the back, through the housing and PCB, into the lid posts. The two at the connector end also hold the clip bracket.
  • Replaceable parcel clip. A separate bracket and hinged jaw with a pin and leaf spring, so the clip can be reprinted or swapped without touching the electronics.
  • Battery swap without soldering. Remove four screws, lift the lid and PCB, unplug the battery and slide the retainer out.
  • Antenna care. Metal screws sit at least 6.6 mm from the ESP32 antenna edge, and nylon screws are specified at the two nearest positions.
  • Charging contacts. Two gold-plated studs line up with the PCB pads and the dock pins on the underside.

05Assembly

A 16-second animation shows the build order, so a person printing the parts can assemble them without a call.

  1. Slide the battery under the hood, wires toward the connector end, and fit the retainer.
  2. Plug the battery into the PCB and seat the board on the four posts.
  3. Place the translucent lid on the housing (tongue and groove).
  4. Set the clip bracket under one end and insert the four screws from the back.
  5. Fit the jaw with the hinge pin and leaf spring.

06Verification

The full assembly was checked in Fusion 360 for interference. No clashes were found between the enclosure parts, PCB, battery, contacts and clip, apart from the intended screw-thread overlaps.

Lid to buzzer
0.40 mm
Lid to LEDs
1.27 mm
Housing to USB-C
0.84 mm
Clip jaw to bracket
0.15 mm
Screw to antenna
6.6 mm or more

These are CAD clearances. The first physical print is what confirms fit, which is why the hand-off lists what to check.

07Hand-off package

  • STL for each of 5 parts
  • STEP for each part
  • One print plate (all 5 parts)
  • Full assembly STEP
  • Editable Fusion 360 files
  • 13 rendered views
  • Assembly animation (MP4)
  • Dimensioned drawings, 4 sheets
  • Bill of materials (CSV)
  • Manufacturing and print notes

Parts are already oriented for printing. Suggested settings: 0.16 to 0.20 mm layers, three walls, 25 to 30% infill. Black PETG, ASA or PA12 for the housing, translucent PETG or frosted resin for the lid, and PA12 or tough resin for the clip jaw.

08Open points, stated up front

A good hand-off says what is not yet proven. These are listed in the package for the first prototype:

  • Height is 23.35 mm, set by the 8.5 mm buzzer and the battery under the PCB. A low-profile buzzer would save about 5 mm.
  • Real battery lead length and how it folds inside the housing.
  • USB-C plug seating in the 2.4 mm recess with the client's cable.
  • Dock pin length and a relief slot in the dock for the clip.
  • Clip spring force and wear, LED light spread through the lid and buzzer loudness.

09Project result

A complete, print-ready enclosure for a parcel beacon, delivered as parts, drawings, a bill of materials and an assembly animation, with the unverified points named so the first prototype can close them.
